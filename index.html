012026  /  MLOps & Governance

Continuous Credit Risk Governance Pipeline

A credit-risk classifier that governs itself. It scores a live batch, waits out a realistic label delay, watches for distribution drift, trains a challenger when the world moves, and promotes it only if the improvement survives a significance test. If the champion decays, it rolls itself back.

  • 01637 batches of the Give Me Some Credit stream run on a GitHub Actions schedule with no human triggering a tick.
  • 02Raising the drift-share threshold from 0.1 to 0.3 cut false retrains from roughly 40% of undrifted batches to about 1%, while still catching real injected drift.
  • 03Promotion needs three gates: a 0.01 AUC-PR tolerance band, strict dominance, and McNemar, or a bootstrap CI when discordant pairs fall below 15.
  • 04Rollback is held to the same bar. A 0.03 AUC-PR drop only counts once the bootstrap CI confirms it, and comparisons are suppressed when the reference window goes stale.
  • 0554 tests across 8 files, including a fixed-seed challenger that looks better purely from small-sample noise and must be rejected.

AUC-PR over accuracy at a 6.7% positive rate. Every decision, including rejections and suppressions, lands in a Postgres audit log, not just the ones that changed something.

022025  /  RAG & Guardrails

Kubernetes Gated RAG

A Kubernetes Q&A system built around defense in depth rather than "LLM plus vector search". Safety and topic gates run before any expensive work, retrieval is threshold-gated instead of merely reordered, and generation fails over across three provider hops.

  • 01Runs end to end on a 16GB laptop with no GPU. Local compute is parsing, chunking and CPU reranking only.
  • 02Both gates fail closed. A classifier error or unparseable verdict blocks the turn instead of letting it through, and verdict parsing reads only the first token.
  • 03Two independent cache layers: exact match in SQLite, then a semantic layer in Qdrant at cosine 0.95 or above. Even a confirmed "no documentation" answer is cached under a short TTL.
  • 04FlashRank reranks, then a hard score floor drops weak chunks entirely. This is the one deliberate fail-open path, chosen as availability over strictness and documented as such.
  • 05Evaluated with RAGAS across six retrieval and generation metrics, judged by a model from a different family than either live chain.

Built as a LangGraph state machine, so every node is independently callable and testable. The ingestion gate deliberately fails open, the inverse of the query-time gates, because a false rejection there silently shrinks a batch job.

032025  /  Agentic Systems

Competitive Intelligence Agent

Give it a company name and it returns a sourced intelligence brief: what the company does, funding and ownership, recent news, competitors and risks. It plans its own sub-questions, criticises its own coverage, and will send itself back to re-plan when a required field is still missing.

  • 01Five required fields, and every claim traces back to a search result or a calculation in the scratchpad. Anything it cannot source is marked "insufficient information" rather than invented.
  • 02Four model families across three providers, chosen so the critic never grades output from a model in its own family and the eval judge is isolated from both.
  • 03Hard stops at 3 replans, 15 tool calls and 8 minutes. On any limit it returns what it confirmed instead of filling gaps with guesses.
  • 04Every Tavily result is wrapped in untrusted-content delimiters before it reaches a prompt, treating open-web text as data and never as instructions.
  • 05A 15-company benchmark runs the whole thing twice, critic loop on and off, and reports the delta. Whether the critic earns its extra latency is measured, not assumed.

Failure is isolated per step: one blocked executor step does not end the run, a critic failure routes straight to the synthesizer, and a synthesizer failure falls back to a scratchpad-only report with no LLM at all.

042025  /  Recommender Systems

ReelBench

Five recommender approaches, from a popularity baseline to a sequential transformer, put through one leakage-tested harness so the comparison actually means something. Accuracy is reported next to coverage and intra-list diversity, because a model that only recommends blockbusters can still look good on Recall alone.

  • 01MovieLens 25M turned into implicit feedback at a rating floor of 4, split on a single global timestamp cutoff and guarded by an assertion that fails the run on leakage.
  • 02Item-item collaborative filtering computed in row blocks over sparse matrices: under 200MB of memory against a projected 15GB for the dense equivalent.
  • 03Candidate generation through FAISS, then a LightGBM re-ranking pass, all on CPU.
  • 04Recall, NDCG and MAP at 10 and 20, plus catalogue coverage and intra-list diversity, so popularity bias is visible rather than hidden.
  • 05A Gemini-built content index handles the cold-start case, served over a simple similarity endpoint.

Stated plainly in the repo: the committed comparison table currently scores the three CPU baselines. The two-tower and sequential models are implemented and runnable, but are not yet in that table, so the numbers are not presented as if they were.

052026  /  Causal Inference

Causal Impact & Heterogeneous Response Analysis

A causal pipeline on a 13.9M-row randomized ad experiment that answers two questions: what the overall effect was, and which audiences actually responded. Before trusting any estimator on real data, it confounds a subsample on purpose and checks whether each method can recover a known ground truth.

  • 01Naive OLS, propensity score matching, IPW and AIPW benchmarked across four confounding severities, producing a bias-severity curve with real bootstrap intervals rather than a single point estimate.
  • 02The outcome variable is chosen by a minimum-detectable-effect calculation, not by default. At 100k per arm, visit can detect about 5.9% relative, conversion only about 24.1%, so conversion is excluded from segment work and the table says why.
  • 03A T-learner CATE model is validated against random targeting with a held-out Qini coefficient, then segments are corrected with Benjamini-Hochberg and checked for whether each one is even large enough to detect the effect claimed.
  • 04Rosenbaum bounds put the critical gamma near 1.20 on the matched pairs. The dashboard reports that as fragile instead of burying it, because only mild unmeasured confounding would overturn the conclusion.
  • 0532 tests, including calibration non-convergence and a check that no control row is ever reused across matched pairs.

Matching is strict 1:1 without replacement, so an 85/15 treated-control split discards most treated units by design. The match rate is surfaced in the dashboard rather than hidden. The single LLM step only interprets diagnostics and cannot touch an estimate, a p-value or a bound.

062026  /  Forecasting & Anomalies

RetailCast

Demand forecasting across 60 retail series, benchmarked the way a business would actually judge it: expanding-window walk-forward validation, scale-free error metrics, and a cost-of-error comparison so the model choice is a money decision rather than a leaderboard one.

  • 01XGBoost wins at MASE 0.632, MAPE 15.23% and WAPE 11.76%, ahead of LightGBM at 0.674, SARIMA at 1.019 and Prophet at 1.001. Both classical models sit above 1.0, meaning they lose to a naive seasonal baseline.
  • 02Validation is expanding-window walk-forward against a held-out 15-day tail, so nothing is scored on data the model could have seen.
  • 03On anomaly detection, simple control limits at k=2.5 beat Isolation Forest at a 5% contamination rate: F1 0.694 against 0.632, driven by much higher recall.
  • 04An illustrative cost-of-error pass prices the residuals: roughly $193k for XGBoost against $206k for LightGBM, which turns a 0.04 MASE gap into a number a stakeholder can act on.
  • 05The narrative report is LLM-written but regex-checked against the computed metrics, and the dashboard prints the resulting grounding score, 96% on the shipped run.

SARIMA's average is over three series rather than all sixty, and that is labelled in the comparison instead of quietly averaged in. The cost figures are explicitly illustrative, built on assumed holding and stockout costs, not on real margins.
